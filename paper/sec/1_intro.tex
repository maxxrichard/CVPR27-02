\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} and SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} classify an image by comparing its embedding with the text embeddings of class names. The resulting zero-shot classifier is general but frozen, and a growing family of methods adapts it from the unlabeled test data itself, transductively on a batch~\cite{martin2024transductive,zanella2024transclip,kalantidis2024zlap,li2025ecalp} or online on a stream~\cite{karmanov2024tda,zhang2024dmn,zhang2024dpe,fuchs2025oga}, without training and on cached features.

\paragraph{Problem.}
These methods are evaluated on batches that contain every class of the vocabulary in comparable numbers. Realistic batches do not: a photo album or a user session contains a handful of the classes that the vocabulary names, and streams are temporally correlated. Under such conditions transductive methods lose tens of accuracy points with respect to the zero-shot model they are meant to improve~\cite{zanella2025stata}, and a controlled benchmark of test-time adaptation reports marginal gains, poor transfer to backbones other than CLIP and degraded calibration~\cite{sheng2025illusion}. The remedy proposed in~\cite{zanella2025stata} and refined in~\cite{huang2026moon} is a statistical anchor: each class prototype is shrunk towards its text embedding with a strength that grows with the number of test samples the class receives. Anchoring prevents collapse but treats every class in isolation: a class that receives no samples stays at its text anchor, although that anchor is displaced from the image distribution by the modality gap and by the domain of the test data~\cite{liang2022gap,schrodi2025two}, and a class with one or two samples is adapted from those samples alone. The problem addressed here is therefore to adapt \emph{all} prototypes of a large vocabulary from an unlabeled batch in which most classes are absent or rare, without collapsing on the absent ones and without discarding what the present ones reveal about them.

\paragraph{Approach.}
The displacement between the text anchor of a class and the centroid of its images contains a component shared by all classes, the modality and domain offset, and a component that varies smoothly with the semantics of the class: breeds of dogs move together, textures move together. \method turns this structure into a prior. The adapted prototypes are written as $\mu_k = t_k + d_k$ and the displacement field $D = [d_1;\dots;d_K]$ receives a Gaussian Markov random field prior whose precision is $\alpha I + \lambda L_T$, where $L_T$ is the Laplacian of a nearest-neighbour graph over the text embeddings. The $\alpha$ term reproduces the anchor-only shrinkage of~\cite{zanella2025stata,huang2026moon}; the $\lambda$ term couples the classes, so that the displacement of a well-observed class propagates to its semantic neighbours. The maximum a posteriori field has a closed form, $D = (N_z + s(\alpha I + \lambda L_T))^{-1} N_z E$ with soft counts $N_z$ and empirical displacements $E$: a graph filter that returns the empirical displacement for well-observed classes and the harmonic extension of the neighbours' displacements for absent ones (\cref{fig:concept}c). Classes that receive no samples are additionally removed from the competition by a Dirichlet-regularised estimate of the class proportions, which treats the vocabulary as a label-shift problem~\cite{saerens2002adjusting,garg2020unified}. All updates are closed form on cached features, and the prior strengths can be selected by maximising the marginal likelihood of the unlabeled batch, which removes the dataset-specific tuning criticised in~\cite{sheng2025illusion}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate transductive VLM adaptation as the estimation of a displacement field over the class vocabulary with a graph-structured prior, and derive closed-form block-coordinate inference for batches and streams (\cref{sec:method}); anchor-only shrinkage and a single global shift are its two extreme settings, and absent classes receive the harmonic extension of the present-class displacements.
  \item We combine the field with an explicit class-presence model and an empirical Bayes rule for the prior strength, so that the vocabulary is treated as a label-shift problem and no pooling strength has to be tuned per dataset.
  \item We evaluate \method under the realistic batch and online protocols on eleven benchmarks and four ImageNet shifts, with architecture-matched controls that isolate the graph coupling and the presence model (\cref{sec:results}), and release a unit-tested implementation that plugs into the official harness.
\end{itemize}
