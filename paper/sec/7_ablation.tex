\section{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Components.}
\Cref{tab:components} adds the components one at a time, in the low setting and on the full test set. Without anchor, coupling or presence the mixture reaches 43.3 in the low setting, 22 points below the zero-shot model, but 70.3 on the full set, where every class is well observed. The presence model alone raises the low-setting result to 55.8 but cannot prevent the present classes from drifting; the anchor term alone raises it to 69.1, and the two together to 69.6, the anchor-only regime of StatA and MOON. The graph coupling adds 1.1 points on top (70.7), the margin that distinguishes \method from the anchored methods, and empirical Bayes a further 0.1. The inherited sample graph contributes 0.3 points in the low setting but 1.5 on the full set, where neighbouring samples carry most of the structure; conversely, the anchor and the coupling change the full-set result by at most 0.4 points, consistent with the special-case analysis of \cref{sec:field}.

\paragraph{Prior and graph.}
We vary $\alpha$, $\lambda$ and the number of neighbours $m$, and replace the text kNN graph by a complete graph and by a degree-matched random graph (table and sensitivity curves in the supplementary material). Accuracy in the low setting varies by at most 0.6 points for $\lambda$ between 1 and 16 and by 0.3 points for $m$ between 3 and 10, so neither is a sensitive hyper-parameter; removing the anchor ($\alpha=0$) costs 1.7 points, removing the coupling ($\lambda=0$) 1.6, and very large values ($\lambda=64$, $\alpha=16$) cost 0.8 and 1.1 points by over-smoothing or over-shrinking the field. The random graph (69.2) performs like no coupling (69.1) and the complete graph (68.8) is below both, so pooling helps only when it follows the semantic neighbourhoods. This is the control that the smoothness hypothesis H1 of \cref{sec:field} requires. H1 itself is tested with labels, independently of any method (plot in the supplementary material): the Dirichlet energy of the oracle displacement field on the text graph, relative to its mean on twenty degree-matched random graphs, lies between 0.25 and 0.75 on all eleven datasets, and the datasets with the highest ratios are among those with the smallest margins in \cref{tab:perdataset}. Likelihood and optimisation variants change the result by at most 0.5 points, and a batch of 64 ImageNet images takes 9 ms against 7 ms for StatA on one GPU (supplementary material).
